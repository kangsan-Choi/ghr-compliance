## Key Data Points (as of 2026-06)

- Minimum wage: $7.25/hr (federal rate; Texas adopts federal by reference, Texas Labor Code §62.051)
- Standard workweek: 40 hours (FLSA); no state cap on total weekly hours
- Overtime: 1.5x for hours over 40/week (FLSA §207); no Texas state overtime law for private sector
- FLSA EAP overtime exemption salary threshold: $684/week ($35,568/yr) — DOL 2024 rule vacated Nov 2024 by Eastern District of Texas
- Annual leave: 0 days legally mandated (no federal or Texas state requirement for private employers)
- Sick leave: HB822 (89th Legislature) — paid sick leave at 1hr per 30hrs worked; effective Sept 1, 2025; applies to new hires Jan 1, 2026 and existing employees accrual starts Jan 1, 2026 (status: enrolled/signed — verify final enactment)
- Termination: At-will employment state; no mandatory notice period; no statutory severance
- Final paycheck: within 6 days of discharge (Texas Payday Law)
- WARN Act: federal only (100+ employees, 60 days notice for plant closing/mass layoff)
- Social Security (FICA): 6.2% employer + 6.2% employee on wages up to $184,500 (2026 wage base)
- Medicare: 1.45% employer + 1.45% employee (no cap); additional 0.9% employee surtax above $200k
- FUTA: 6.0% federal, effective 0.6% after 5.4% SUTA credit (on first $7,000/employee)
- Texas SUTA: $9,000 wage base; new employer rate 2.70%; RTR 0.21%; OA 0.00% (2026)

## Notable Recent Developments

- Nov 2024: Eastern District of Texas vacated Biden DOL overtime rule (salary threshold stays at $684/wk)
- Jan 2025: SCOTUS E.M.D. Sales v. Carrera — preponderance standard applies for FLSA exemption proof (employer-friendly)
- Sept 2025: Texas noncompete restrictions tightened for healthcare practitioners (physicians, nurses, PAs, dentists)
- Jan 2026: Texas AI regulation law effective — antidiscrimination/disclosure requirements for AI in hiring
- Jan 2026: HB3699 — clarifies unemployment compensation "last work" definition

## Texas Characteristics for Foreign Companies

- Strong at-will employment state (no implied covenant of good faith)
- No state income tax (reduces administrative burden)
- Cities CANNOT set local minimum wages higher than state/federal (state preemption)
- No state disability insurance mandate (unlike CA, NY, NJ, HI)
- Workers' compensation is elective for most private employers (but non-subscribers face liability exposure)
